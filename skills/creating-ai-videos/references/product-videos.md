# Product Videos

## Purpose

Help viewers decide whether a product solves a real problem and what to do next. Demonstration is the main evidence; the creator's broader AI worldview belongs in a separate viewpoint video. A product-level reason may explain a feature or design boundary, but do not turn it into an industry-wide thesis or opinion-leader monologue.

## Recommended structure

1. **Real problem:** Show the task or frustration in the viewer's language.
2. **Result first:** Reveal the completed outcome before explaining setup.
3. **Critical path:** Record the shortest complete workflow from input to result.
4. **Evidence:** Show actual output, time saved, comparison, or repeatable behavior.
5. **Limits:** State failures, prerequisites, platform limits, privacy concerns, and who should not use it.
6. **Audience:** Say who benefits and under what conditions.
7. **Action:** Provide the official page, repository, installer, documentation, or next step.

For the creator's own product, say that it is the creator's work. If the video has sponsorship, affiliate income, gifts, or another commercial relationship, make that relationship visible.

## Script contract

A product script should contain:

- one-sentence viewer problem;
- one-sentence result promise that the demo can prove;
- ordered recording steps;
- spoken explanation for each critical action;
- observed result and test conditions;
- limitations and suitable audience;
- one clear call to action.

Do not insert an industry-history essay, an unrelated future prediction, or a claim that one successful run proves universal reliability.

## Visual priority

Use the actual product, cursor actions, inputs, outputs, before/after states, official documentation, and concise evidence cards. Keep decorative footage secondary.

## Product-video review

Check that the viewer can answer: What problem does it solve? Did I see it work? What does it require? Where can it fail? Is it for me? How do I get it?

# Viewpoint Videos

## Purpose

Give viewers a useful map and a defensible judgment, not a disguised product promotion or an exhaustive technical lecture.

## Narrative model

1. **Shared memory:** Start with a product, event, meme, or experience the audience recognizes.
2. **Knowledge gap:** Show why the familiar explanation is incomplete.
3. **Main judgment:** State or strongly imply the single idea the video will prove.
4. **Timeline:** Move through a small number of decisive stages or turning points.
5. **Hidden line:** Track the capability, incentive, relationship, or constraint changing beneath the events.
6. **Mechanism:** Explain why the observed change happened.
7. **Present location:** Tell viewers which stage they are in now.
8. **Outlook:** Separate supported direction from uncertain prediction.
9. **Closing:** Return to the main judgment in the creator's own language.

## Evidence model

Build a fact card for every major claim:

- claim;
- primary source;
- publication or test date;
- what the source directly proves;
- what remains inference;
- visual evidence to show on screen.

Prefer official announcements, original speeches, product documentation, papers, repositories, filings, and the creator's own tests. Use secondary reporting to find leads and competing interpretations.

## AI-evolution series

For stage overviews, use recognizable products as anchors while keeping the technical history accurate. A current working sequence is Chatbot → stronger reasoning and tool use → Agent or Coding Agent → possible future systems with longer-lived memory, continual learning, self-correction, or greater autonomy.

Track capability lines across stages, for example:

- prompt → reusable skill or procedure;
- context window → managed context and memory;
- answer generation → tool use and task execution;
- one-off interaction → longer-running work;
- static model behavior → adaptation, feedback, and possible continual learning.

Do not treat one product as the definition or sole beginning of a technical stage. Label future routes as outlook or inference.

## Style reference boundary

Borrow the research discipline, familiar entry point, timeline, relationship chain, evidence, narrative tension, and high-level closing associated with strong documentary-style creators. Do not reproduce their catchphrases, sentence rhythm, narrator persona, music, footage, graphics, or script passages.

## Viewpoint-video review

Check that the viewer can answer: What did I already recognize? What changed? What mechanism explains it? Which claims are facts? What is the creator's judgment? What remains uncertain?
